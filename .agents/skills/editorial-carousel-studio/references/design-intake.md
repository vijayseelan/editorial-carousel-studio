# Design intake and variability

## Ask only what matters

Do not force a questionnaire when the creator has already supplied a clear direction. Infer ordinary defaults and confirm only decisions that would significantly change the outcome.

For an open brief, establish:

- mood: documentary, archival, urgent, optimistic, confrontational or neutral;
- palette: midnight, cream, campaign red, sky blue, clean white or custom;
- density: spare, balanced or information-rich;
- cover: editorial portrait, conceptual scene, collage, archival treatment or typography-led;
- motion: none, minimal, balanced or motion-rich;
- typography and brand constraints;
- preferred mix of narrative, data and diagram slides.

## Offer directions when needed

Give up to three directions. Each direction should name the mood, palette, cover treatment, typography character, diagram language and motion behaviour. Avoid presenting superficial colour-only variants.

Recommend one direction and explain how it supports the approved narrative. The creator may combine elements.

## Controlled variability

Vary the following within approved systems:

- theme tokens;
- two or three layout families appropriate to the content;
- semantic visual metaphors such as timelines, networks, ladders, loops, comparisons and bars;
- motion recipes such as draw, reveal, connect, count and highlight;
- cover illustration prompt and crop.

Do not randomize typography, spacing, icon style or motion timing slide by slide. Variability should make different projects distinct without making one carousel incoherent.

## Storyboard review

Before full production, show the creator:

- slide number and purpose;
- draft headline or message, based only on approved claims;
- proposed visual component;
- static or animated format;
- evidence claim IDs;
- source-footer plan.

This review is separate from the earlier evidence approval.
